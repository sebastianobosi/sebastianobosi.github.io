Jet HR

The redesign of the Jet HR employee area, on desktop and mobile, and a payslip you can understand even without knowing the abbreviations.

In short

Problem

The app is a shrunk-down desktop and the payslip speaks the language of the payroll consultant.

My role

Solo project: discovery, regulatory research, UX, UI, design system and test plan.

What I did

Web app and mobile app with the same components, a new payslip document and a payslip explained line by line.

Outcome

An employee area that answers the most frequent questions straight away, ready for user testing.

The project

Jet HR runs payroll for many small Italian companies. The employee area is where people check their payslip, request time off, upload receipts and download documents. Today the mobile app is a shrunk-down version of the desktop, and the payslip speaks the language of the payroll consultant. I redesigned both starting from one question: what does an employee really want to know when they open the app?

The right questions

People who open the employee area almost always have a specific question. I ranked the most frequent ones and built the home and the payslip to answer those first.

  • How much do I get, and when?

    Net pay and payment date are the first thing on the home screen. Net pay stays hidden until you tap the eye.

  • Why is it different from last month?

    Every payslip says what changed from the previous month, with the line items that explain the difference.

  • How many days off do I have?

    Holidays and leave in days, with hours as a detail and the estimate at 31 December.

  • Where does the money go between gross and net?

    A bar split in three: what you keep, pension contributions and taxes.

What can be changed

Before redesigning the payslip I checked what is mandatory and what isn’t. The form is free, the content isn’t.

  • Free form

    The payslip has no fixed template: it can be reorganised, as long as it contains all the data.

  • Same data, new words

    Amounts and codes stay the official ones: only the way they read changes.

  • Privacy first

    Personal data protected by default: the salary stays hidden until you want to see it.

  • Transparency

    From the profile you can request pay information for your own role.

The web app

The home shows the salary with its payment date, time off in days, to-dos and the team’s week. Requests are made from a side panel, without losing the calendar.

The mobile app

System elements stay native, so the app feels familiar straight away. Inside are the same components as the web app: moving from computer to phone, you find the same rows, the same cards and the same buttons.

Decision

In the bottom bar the sections use Jet HR’s own icons, the same as on desktop: the brand is recognisable everywhere.

The payslip

The official PDF remains the legal document, but reorganised around questions. In the app, the explained view puts every line item into plain words, with the official name and code alongside. The amounts are the same; the words change.

Accessibility and testing

  • Legible

    Contrast checked on the brand colours, so amounts and dates read at first glance.

  • Easy to use

    Large touch targets and actions always in the same place, on desktop and mobile.

  • Clear for everyone

    Plain words instead of abbreviations, for people who don’t know payroll jargon.

Next validation

Usability tests with employees on desktop and mobile, on everyday tasks: understanding how much you get paid and when, explaining a payslip line item, requesting a day off, uploading an expense, finding the annual tax certificate.

What I learned

Studying the legal constraints before designing changed the project: the form of the payslip is free, the content isn’t. That told me exactly how far I could go.

I would make the same choice again to use the same components on desktop and mobile: the experience is consistent and the design system stays small.

Next time I would put employees’ questions in front of real people earlier, instead of starting only from public sources.

Next steps

Usability tests with real employees, copy review with a payroll consultant, dark mode and Dynamic Type, handoff to developers.
