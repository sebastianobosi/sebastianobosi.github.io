idealista

A new brand identity and the app that grows out of it: finding a home with clear prices and verified listings, from search to viewing.

Role
Brand and UX/UI Designer · personal project
Platform
Mobile app
Tools
Figma

All work

In short

Problem

Unclear prices, fear of fake listings and rigid filters make choosing a home hard.

My role

Solo project: brand identity, discovery, information architecture, UI, design system and brand video.

What I did

An evolutionary rebrand, 54 icons, 39 screens across 7 flows and a design system with dark mode.

Outcome

Total monthly cost in plain sight, natural-language search and anti-scam warnings in chat.

The project

I rethought idealista starting from the brand: the pixel-grid logo becomes a real typeface, and the square pixel stays as the mark of the whole system. From there I redesigned the app starting from the current website, with one goal: make it immediately clear how much a home really costs and whom to trust.

Process

From the brand to the app, with the same tokens from the first step to the last.

The system

Discovery

Unclear prices

The rent doesn’t tell you what you’ll really spend: service charges, heating and deposit come later, often in chat.

Trust to be earned

Fake listings and requests for a deposit before the viewing are the most cited fear in reviews.

Rigid search

There are many filters and they are hidden. People search in sentences: “three-room flat with balcony in Isola under €1,400”.

Limitation

Discovery based on desk research: website, app stores and public reviews. The assumptions need validating with usability tests and interviews with people looking for and renting out homes.

Architecture

Navigation with the labels every property app uses. “Publish” gathers what the website scatters across its home page: your listings, idealista/valuta, mortgages and agencies.

  1. Home
  2. Saved
  3. Messages
  4. Publish
  5. Profile

Search the way you speak

Search. The sentence becomes visible, editable filters. The trust filters, “Verified only” and “Exclude auctions”, are chips always in view. On the map, the pixel shows what is new, selected or already seen.

What you really spend

Listing and contact. Above the fold: price, €/m² and total monthly cost including service charges and heating. A single CTA, “Contact”. The message is ready to send and the viewing is booked in the app.

Safety at the moment of risk

Saved searches and safety. Saved searches stay alive: change a filter and they update by themselves, with Undo. If someone in chat asks for money before the viewing, the app warns you, hides the IBAN and lets you report it in two taps.

Owners know the price

Publish. Publishing a listing in 5 steps, with an automatic draft and the address private by default. The price comes from idealista/valuta, and identity verification earns the “Verified” badge.

Design system

Brand and app speak the same language: colours, spacing and components come from the same tokens, in light and dark mode, and every component is documented.

  • A single magenta action per screen: it is always the primary CTA.

  • Active state as in the brand: black tile, lime pixel above the icon, white label.

  • Dark mode comes from the same tokens, without duplicating components.

Accessibility

  • Contrast checked

    In light and dark mode, with lime used as a surface and never as text.

  • Easy to tap

    Chips, map pins and buttons designed for the thumb.

  • Clear states

    Every state uses more than one signal: colour, icon or text.

What I learned

An evolutionary rebrand is harder than starting from scratch: the real work is deciding what to keep. Keeping only the square pixel gave consistency to the logo, icons, map and motion.

I would keep the rule of a single magenta action per screen: it simplified dozens of interface decisions.

I would change the order: I would run a few interviews before closing the flows. Reviews tell you what doesn’t work, not always why.

Next steps

Usability tests, interviews with private landlords, real photos instead of placeholders and handoff to developers.
